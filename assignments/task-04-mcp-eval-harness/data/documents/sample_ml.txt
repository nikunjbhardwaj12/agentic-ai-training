Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is the technique used to align large
language models with human preferences. It consists of three main stages:

1. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT): A pretrained base language model is fine-tuned on a
   curated dataset of high-quality human demonstrations, teaching it to follow
   instructions and produce well-formed responses.

2. Reward model training: Human labelers rank multiple model outputs for the same
   prompt from best to worst. These rankings are used to train a separate reward
   model that learns to predict a scalar score representing human preference for
   any given response.

3. Reinforcement learning via PPO: The fine-tuned language model is further optimized
   using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that
   updates the model's policy to maximize the reward predicted by the reward model,
   while staying close to the original fine-tuned policy.

Why PPO instead of vanilla policy gradient methods

Vanilla policy gradient methods update the policy directly using the raw gradient of
expected reward, which can lead to very large, destabilizing updates — a single bad
update can cause catastrophic policy collapse, where the model's behavior degrades
sharply and does not recover.

PPO addresses this with a clipped surrogate objective. Instead of allowing unlimited
policy updates, PPO clips the ratio between the new and old policy probabilities within
a small range (e.g., 0.8 to 1.2), which prevents the policy from moving too far in a
single step. This clipping mechanism gives PPO much greater training stability than
vanilla policy gradient methods, and is the main reason it is preferred for the
reinforcement learning stage of RLHF.

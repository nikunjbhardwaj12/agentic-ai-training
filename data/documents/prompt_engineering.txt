Prompt engineering has evolved from basic heuristic instruction writing into a rigorous architectural discipline for maximizing the reasoning capacities of Large Language Models. Advanced frameworks rely heavily on structured prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), where the model is explicitly instructed to generate intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. 

Beyond single-turn reasoning, modern agentic design relies on automated Self-Reflection loops. In a reflection framework, an Evaluator agent critiques a generated draft using structured output matching specified evaluation rubrics. The critique assesses dimensions such as faithfulness—verifying if claims are anchored strictly in the provided context—and relevance to the initial request. 

If the draft fails to meet numerical quality thresholds, the pipeline redirects control backward in the execution cycle. The feedback and actionable critique are passed back into the prompt of the planning or writing agents. The system then re-plans or re-drafts dynamically, iterating up to a pre-configured maximum iteration safety cap to prevent endless execution loops while ensuring production-grade output quality.
